## Core argument

In *Theory of International Politics* (1979), Kenneth Waltz proposed a **systemic** theory: to explain recurring patterns such as the balance of power, it is enough to look at the structure of the system. Since there is no central authority, states act through **self-help**; what sets them apart is how much power they have.

## Strands

**Defensive realism** (Waltz) holds that states seek enough security and that overreaching provokes counter-coalitions. **Offensive realism** (Mearsheimer) argues that, under uncertainty, the best guarantee is to become the dominant power in one's own region.

## Critiques and legacy

The peaceful end of the Cold War, which neorealism did not predict, opened space for constructivist approaches. The theory remains central to debates on bipolarity, unipolarity and competition between the United States and China.
